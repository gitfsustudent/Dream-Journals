The legend

In 2006 a sketch of an ordinary man went around the web. The claim: thousands of people, in cities all over the world, see this same face in their dreams.

It is widely regarded as a hoax. A vague, average face resembles someone you have met, and memory fills in the rest.

Be honest: does this face feel familiar?

Real dreams are less mysterious, and more specific. So we read two journals.

Every window below is one real dream, written down the morning after. Read each floor left to right, then go down. The color is the feeling the dream is mostly about.

    Does the mood change?

    average feeling per 100 dreams, length-adjusted

    Each dot is the average for one year of Barb's journal, or one stretch of about 70 dreams for Pegasus. The shaded band is the range we are 95% sure of: if it is wide, the data cannot say much. Anger comes first because it is the clearest change in Barb's journal. The vertical scale is the same for both journals within a feeling, but the two people are decades and lives apart, so compare the direction of each line, not how high they sit.

    Amber means the trend line's 95% range excludes zero. With five feelings tested, one or two of these could be chance; in Barb's journal only anger survives a strict correction. Pegasus has no dates, so his lines assume the order of his dreams is the order he had them.
